Daytime Syndication Is Losing Real Estate

By Terry Bryant · PR STARPOWER Press Room · Beat 1: The Pulse · August 16, 2026

HOLLYWOOD, Calif. — Two syndicated daytime talk shows are ending within months of each other this year, and each has been covered as its own story. Put next to each other, they're one story.

Confirmed reporting, not rumor: The Kelly Clarkson Show will conclude after its seventh season, airing through fall 2026 — Kelly Clarkson's own decision to step away from hosting a talk show, per Deadline and Variety. Separately, Sherri, hosted by Sherri Shepherd, has been canceled after four seasons, with final episodes airing through fall 2026; distributor Debmar-Mercury is reportedly exploring a digital continuation, though nothing there is confirmed.

The connection worth drawing: these are two of syndicated daytime's more recently launched entrants — not legacy institutions with decades of run — both gone in the same year. That's not the same signal as one long-running show ending on its own timeline. Two comparatively young shows closing in the same twelve months reads as a field getting smaller, not two unrelated business decisions.

Neither departure is framed publicly as a response to the other, and there's no confirmed reporting connecting the two decisions causally — that link is this desk's own read, not a claim either production has made.

What this means for PR STARPOWER

A shrinking field changes where a booking actually belongs. As syndicated daytime real estate contracts, the shows with genuinely open, standing submission processes — not gated behind an existing relationship — become more valuable relative to the ones closing, not less. That's a practical read for anyone doing daytime booking work right now, not just a media-industry observation.

Sourcing: Kelly Clarkson Show conclusion via Deadline and Variety (both reporting Clarkson's own decision); Sherri cancellation via Variety, Deadline, and the Atlanta Journal-Constitution.
