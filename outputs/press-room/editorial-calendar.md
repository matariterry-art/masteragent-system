## Ranked slate

### 1. [Beat 3 — AI/tech] "The Sora Shutdown: What It Signals About the AI Video Shakeout"
**Angle:** OpenAI is discontinuing Sora's web and app entirely (already done, April 26, 2026) and
its API on September 24, 2026 — inside this planning window. Meanwwhile Google's Veo 3 and Runway
have become the tools studios actually build workflows around. The original connection this desk
can draw that isn't being drawn elsewhere: the highest-hype AI video model is being wound down at
the exact moment the *less flashy, workflow-integrated* tools are winning real studio adoption —
which cuts against the "AI hype cycle" narrative and toward a "utility beats novelty" one.
**Bucket discipline:** Runway/Veo = shipped and usable today (real studio workflows exist).
Anything past that (e.g. next-gen model previews) gets sorted separately, not blended in.
**Why ranked #1:** priority beat, genuinely original angle, real dated news peg inside the window,
both-sides framing is natural (opportunity for firms/talent who adapt workflows vs. risk for
anyone whose whole strategy was chasing the highest-hype tool).
**Sourcing status:** solid — OpenAI's own discontinuation dates, plus converging trade/industry
coverage on studio tool adoption patterns. Ready to draft.
**What's needed from the founder:** nothing to start drafting; a point-of-view check once drafted,
since this piece explicitly takes a position (utility over novelty).
**Demonstration file produced this run:** see `beat-1-pulse/` note below — actually built as the
Beat 1 lead instead, this item is fully specified but not yet rendered as HTML.

### 3. [Beat 2 — The Business of the Business] "Who Has Power This Quarter: The Warner Bros. Discovery Endgame"
**Angle:** a live, unresolved fight for Warner Bros. Discovery — Netflix's own all-cash-and-stock
bid (~$82.7B enterprise value, per one source) versus a competing Paramount Skydance bid (~$81B,
per a separate source, which includes a September 30, 2026 "ticking fee" deadline mechanism and a
$7B termination fee if the deal collapses on regulatory grounds). This is exactly a "who has power
this quarter" story — the outcome reshapes which studio executives, production relationships, and
IP libraries matter most for the next several years.
**Why ranked #3, not higher:** real stakes and real differentiation, but there's a genuine sourcing
conflict that has to be resolved before this can run — see below. Ranking it third rather than
first reflects that it needs a verification pass, not that the story is weak.
**Sourcing status — NOT yet ready, flagged honestly:** two searches this run surfaced two
different accounts of which bid is currently leading (Netflix's own filing vs. reporting on a
"superior" Paramount Skydance counter-bid). This could mean the situation moved between the two
sources' publish dates, or it could mean one source is stale. **Do not publish this until that
conflict is resolved with a fresh, dated check** — this is precisely the "mark unverified, date
every verification" discipline this desk exists to enforce, including on itself.
**What's needed from the founder:** nothing yet — this needs another research pass before it's
draft-ready, not a founder decision.
