## Why daytime talk, not late night, is the natural first lane
Late night skews younger, music-forward-but-not-legacy, and interview segments are short and
comedy-adjacent. Daytime talk shows built around conversation, women's-interest audiences, and
"life story" segments are a much closer fit for a legacy artist with a memoir-adjacent book and a
faith-forward single — the format rewards exactly the kind of long-arc, personal-history
storytelling this pitch has. That's a strategic read, not just a category label — flagging it per
standing rule 13.

## Daytime talk
- **Tamron Hall Show** — has a public submission process for guests/stories via
  TamronHallShow.com, run by Summerdale Productions. Per their own terms-of-submission page,
  submissions are evaluated by producers; no payment for use, standard media-release terms apply.
  This is a real, published route — not a guess. **Correct department:** show producers via the
  official submission portal, not general show contact.
  *(Source: [Terms of Submission — Tamron Hall Show](https://tamronhallshow.com/terms-of-submission/))*
- **The Kelly Clarkson Show, The View, Sherri** — not independently researched this run; flagged
  as next targets for the same submission-route research, given the same daytime-talk fit.

## Faith/gospel-specific television — a distinct, high-fit lane
- **The Word Network** — described as the largest African-American religious network globally,
  with an established history of gospel artist interviews, videos, and musical specials (past
  guests cited include Kirk Franklin, CeCe Winans, Marvin Sapp, Donnie McClurkin). This is a
  strong fit for the gospel single specifically — a much lower barrier to entry than mainstream
  daytime talk, and an audience that already cares about gospel chart activity.
  *(Source: [About the Word Network](https://www.thewordnetwork.org/about-twn/))*
- **Bounce TV** — targets African American adult audiences with a mix including inspirational,
  faith-based programming (e.g. gospel-performance-focused segments like "BSJ's Back Stage Pass").
  Worth investigating as a secondary faith/legacy-audience outlet.
  *(Source: search-result summary of Bounce TV programming — not independently fetched this run.)*
