# Industry Intelligence — Focused Brief: EPK Standards for Legacy Artists

**Run date:** August 16, 2026
**Scope:** Focused brief, not the full five-area weekly sweep — requested to inform building
Freda Payne's EPK against real standards. Covers: current EPK format, what's inside a top-tier
EPK and in what order, what bookers/segment producers actually want (and what gets them to
ignore something), what's changed in the last two years, and legacy-vs-new-artist differences.

**Sourcing honesty check, up front:** everything below is aggregated from independent
industry-guide and how-to content (AMW, Chartlex, Reelcrafter, Prezly, Artist Results, and
similar) — consistent across multiple independent sources, which is why it's labeled "confirmed
reporting" rather than rumor. But none of this is a direct quote from a named booker, a named
segment producer, or primary trade press (Billboard, Variety, THR) confirming these are their
personal standards. Treat this as strong, converging industry consensus — not a verified
first-person account. Worth a follow-up pass that tries to find primary-sourced material (an
actual Billboard/Variety piece quoting a real booker) if this becomes a recurring decision point.

## 1. Format: web link, not PDF — CONFIRMED (converging industry consensus)

Every source pulled in this pass agrees on the same shift: the current standard is a **living web
page**, not a PDF attachment. A PDF that goes out over email gets stale the moment anything about
the artist changes; a web page can be updated once and stays current for every future pitch.

The consistent best-practice pattern found: **primary format is a web page** (on the artist's or
agency's own site), with a **secondary, condensed one-page PDF** kept on hand specifically for
cold email attachments, where a clickable link inside an email body sometimes gets clipped or
ignored.
*(Sources: [AMW EPK Guide](https://amworldgroup.com/blog/electronic-press-kit), [Artistrack —
SEO-Rich EPK 2026](https://artistrack.com/create-perfect-seo-rich-epk-2026/), [Presspage — How to
create a press kit in 2026](https://presspage.com/blog/how-to-create-a-press-kit))*

**What this means for PR STARPOWER:** the EPK for Freda needs a real, permanent web page as its
primary form — not just a Word/PDF file sitting in `outputs/`. That's a hosting decision the
founder needs to make (a page on a PR STARPOWER site, a dedicated subdomain, or a service built
for this) before the EPK is truly "delivery-ready" — a finished document isn't the same as a
finished EPK if there's nowhere permanent for it to live. `client-desk` should treat the PDF as
the secondary, cold-email-only format, not the primary deliverable.

## 2. What's inside, and in what order — CONFIRMED (converging consensus)

Recommended order, consistent across sources: **name and one-line hook → short bio → embedded
music/video → photos → press quotes and stats → tour/appearance dates → contact info.** The logic
given for this order: it mirrors how a booker actually reads a pitch — hook first, proof last.

Bio length: sources converge on roughly 300 words, with the expectation that a booker skims the
whole kit in under 30-60 seconds. Multiple sources specifically recommend having **three bio
lengths ready** (a short one-liner for social profiles, a medium version, a full version) rather
than one bio used everywhere.

**A specific, repeated rule worth flagging directly: if a section would be empty or thin, cut it
entirely rather than leaving it half-filled.** Sources are explicit that a blank or "coming soon"
section reads worse than not having the section at all.
*(Sources: [Chartlex — Build a Music Press Kit 2026](https://www.chartlex.com/blog/career/how-to-build-a-music-press-kit-epk-2026),
[Reelcrafter — EPKs 101](https://www.reelcrafter.com/blog/epks-101-what-every-artist-needs-in-their-electronic-press-kits),
[About My Sound — 2026 Musician EPK Checklist](https://aboutmysound.com/blog-posts/the-2026-musician-epk-checklist-what-booking-agents-actually-want-to-see))*

**What this means for PR STARPOWER:** this directly changes how the EPK should be sequenced —
hook and bio first, not credits-first the way a traditional one-sheet sometimes runs. It also
means client-desk's current gap (Freda's Assets section — approved photos, approved bio — is
still blank) is a harder blocker than it might seem: a bio and photos aren't just "nice to have"
inputs, they're two of the first three things a booker sees. And per the empty-section rule,
Team/Will-and-will-not/Assets being blank in her client file isn't just an internal gap — those
sections, if built into a public-facing EPK unfinished, would actively hurt the pitch rather than
just being incomplete.

## 3. What gets a submission looked at — versus ignored — CONFIRMED (converging consensus)

Consistent findings across sources on what a booker actually does when reviewing submissions:
- They give a pitch **about 30 seconds** before moving on, and will listen to **30-60 seconds** of
  one track, not a full song.
- **Low-resolution or unclear photos** are named repeatedly as a fast rejection trigger — group
  shots where it's unclear who the artist is, heavy filters, or images that don't match the
  music's tone.
- **A kit requiring multiple separate downloaded attachments rarely gets opened past the first
  one.** This reinforces the web-page-over-PDF finding above — it's not just a format preference,
  it's a direct cause of pitches getting ignored.
- **Generic, mass-blasted pitches get filtered out** — sources describe experienced talent buyers
  as able to "easily separate serious applicants from those simply firing off emails."
- **Outdated stats undermine trust faster than low numbers do.** A kit with a stale figure reads
  as worse than a kit that simply shows modest, current numbers.
*(Sources: [Stagent — What is an EPK](https://stagent.com/blog/what-is-an-epk), [Gigwell — How to
Book a Show](https://www.gigwell.com/blog/how-to-book-shows), [Chartlex 2026 EPK
guide](https://www.chartlex.com/blog/career/how-to-build-a-music-press-kit-epk-2026))*

**What this means for PR STARPOWER:** this validates decisions already built into the system —
`media-desk`'s bespoke-pitch lane (real personalization, not a mass blast) and `booking-desk`'s
"never invent a contact" discipline are both aligned with what actually gets a pitch opened, not
ignored. It also gives client-desk a concrete, testable bar for Freda's EPK: does it load and make
sense in under a minute, on a phone, without downloading anything separate? If not, it will be
skimmed and closed exactly like the sources describe.

## 4. What's changed in the last two years — CONFIRMED, with one item flagged UNVERIFIED

- The shift from "PDF attachment" to "living web page as part of a digital newsroom" is described
  consistently as a recent change, not a long-standing norm — multiple sources frame 2026 EPK
  practice explicitly against "how it used to work" (email a static PDF, rebuild it per campaign).
- **A newer, more speculative shift: press kits being built for AI discoverability, not just human
  journalists** — sources describe "AI Engine Optimization" (AEO) as an emerging practice, and
  reference press kits needing to be readable by AI-powered search/answer tools, not only people.
  **This is UNVERIFIED as an established practice** — it reads more as an emerging framing from
  marketing-adjacent sources than a confirmed, widespread industry standard yet. Flagging it
  because it's the kind of early signal `industry-intelligence` exists to catch, not because it's
  settled.
*(Sources: [Presspage 2026 guide](https://presspage.com/blog/how-to-create-a-press-kit),
[ALM Corp — Journalism Trends 2026](https://almcorp.com/blog/journalism-media-technology-trends-2026-complete-guide/))*

**What this means for PR STARPOWER:** the web-page shift is real enough to act on now. The
AI-discoverability angle is not — worth a dedicated future full sweep (this was a focused brief,
not the five-area weekly one) rather than building Freda's EPK around a trend that isn't confirmed
as standard practice yet.

## 5. Legacy artist EPK versus new artist EPK — the direct answer

A legacy/heritage artist's EPK needs several things a new artist's kit doesn't, per the one
source that spoke to this directly:
- **Tour/performance history with real specifics** — cities, venues, capacity — not just upcoming
  dates. A new artist has no history to show; a legacy artist's history *is* part of the pitch.
- **Key placements with real numbers** — playlist placements, chart history, and similar, where
  the numbers themselves are part of the credibility case.
- **Label or publishing affiliations, where relevant** — a new artist usually doesn't have this
  layer yet.
- **A compiled live-highlight reel (60-90 seconds)** pulled from a real performance history,
  rather than a single current live video — this only works if there's enough past footage to
  select from.
- **A more selective, curated press section** — 3-5 strongest quotes with outlet logos, rather
  than a long running list. The framing given: a legacy artist likely has *more* press to choose
  from, not less, so the skill is curation, not accumulation.
*(Source: [AMW EPK Guide](https://amworldgroup.com/blog/electronic-press-kit) — this was the only
source in this pass that addressed established-artist EPKs directly and specifically; treat this
section as based on one source, not the broader converging-consensus standard the other findings
above have.)*

**What this means for PR STARPOWER:** this is a genuinely good fit for Freda — six decades of
touring history, real chart data (Grammy nomination, Rhythm and Blues Hall of Fame, NAACP Image
Award nomination — see `clients/freda-payne.md`), and a large body of press to curate from rather
than accumulate. The gap isn't raw material, it's packaging discipline: the instinct with someone
this accomplished is to include everything; the actual standard calls for picking the best 3-5
credits and quotes, not the complete list. That's a real tension client-desk should watch for when
this EPK actually gets built.
