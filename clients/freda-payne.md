FLAG — read before pitching anything book-related: Freda already has a published memoir, "Band
of Gold: A Memoir" (with Mark Bego, introduction by the late Mary Wilson, Yorkshire Publishing,
November 2, 2021) — well-reviewed at the time by SoulTracks and Digital Journal, among others.
This is a different, already-public book from the in-development "The Last Glamour Girl" below.
How the two relate — a second book, a follow-up, a different format or angle — is not something
research can determine. This needs to be settled before any pitch describes "The Last Glamour
Girl" without addressing the existing memoir, or a journalist who already covered the 2021 book
will notice the gap immediately.
[Source: Biblio, Digital Journal, SoulTracks, Amazon listing, The NY Independent]

Early life and career start: born September 19, 1942, Detroit, Michigan. Older sister of Scherrie
Payne, final lead singer of The Supremes. Toured with Pearl Bailey's musical revue and sang with
the Duke Ellington Band after high school; appeared on The Tonight Show, The Merv Griffin Show,
and The Dick Cavett Show in the 1960s.
[Source: TheHistoryMakers biography, Wikipedia]

Key recordings and discography:
- First album, "After the Lights Go Down" (Impulse Records/ABC, 1962).
- "Band of Gold" (Invictus Records, 1970) — her signature hit and first gold record: US #3, UK #1
  for six weeks. Written by Holland-Dozier-Holland (credited as "Edythe Wayne" due to a dispute
  with Motown) and Ron Dunbar.
- "Bring the Boys Home" (1971) — Vietnam-era protest song, US #12, her second gold record.
- "Contact" (1971 album) — Grammy-nominated. Exact category UNVERIFIED this pass.
- Left Invictus in 1973; signed to Capitol Records in 1977, releasing three disco albums through
  1979.
- 21 albums recorded to date, per her own artist bio — self-reported figure, not independently
  itemized in this research pass.
- "Let There Be Love!" — jazz vocal album; basis for her 2022 NAACP Image Award nomination (see
  Awards below).
- "Just to Be With You" — contemporary single produced by former Motown producer Michael B.
  Sutton; sources place an initial release in 2022 with a "Golden Promises Mix" version covered
  again in 2024-2025 press. Exact release-date sequencing UNVERIFIED — sources disagree slightly.
[Source: TheHistoryMakers, Wikipedia ("Band of Gold" song and album pages), Songfacts,
Every UK Number 1, The Urban Music Scene, SoulTracks]

Stage and Broadway credits:
- Broadway debut as understudy to Leslie Uggams in "Hallelujah Baby" (1967).
- "Ain't Misbehavin'" (touring/regional).
- "Jelly's Last Jam" (alongside Gregory Hines and Savion Glover).
- "Blues in the Night" — starring role in a 2007 revival at the Post Street Theatre, San
  Francisco, with Maurice Hines, Carol Woods, and Paulette Ivory. Note: the original 1983
  Broadway production of "Blues in the Night" received a Tony nomination for Best Musical, but
  that was before Payne's documented involvement — her credit found in this research is the 2007
  revival cast, not the Tony-nominated original run. Do not conflate the two.
- Equity Theatre production of "Lost in the Stars."
- "A Tribute to Ella Fitzgerald" — a solo concert program, performed at multiple venues including
  54 Below (New York, April 2024) and Marin Center (February 2024) — her most consistently active
  live vehicle in recent press.
